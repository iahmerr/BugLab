You flushed a cockroach. Problem solved, right? Not quite. A cockroach can hold its breath for up to 40 minutes, and the pipe under your toilet leads straight to its favourite place on Earth.

In this episode of Bugs Lab we follow one flushed cockroach all the way down: what happens to a cockroach after you flush it, where the water really takes it, why the sewer is a five-star hotel for the American cockroach, how scientists caught roaches climbing back into homes, and what actually keeps them out of yours.

🪳 What you'll learn
• How a cockroach survives being flushed (and how long it can stay underwater)
• What scientists found when they searched a city's sewers
• How roaches squeeze through a crack thinner than a coin
• Why they're so hard to catch, squash or starve
• Simple, proven pest control tips to stop a cockroach in the toilet or drain from coming back

🔬 Every fact in this video comes from real research
• Holding breath up to ~40 min, ~1 week without water: Terminix; PestWorld (National Pest Management Association)
• Tyler, Texas sewer study (about 40% of manholes infested, roaches entering homes up to a block away and passing plumbing traps): "Studies on Cockroaches in a Municipal Sewerage System"
• Penang, Malaysia sewer study (4,894 cockroaches marked in three sewers): Journal of Medical Entomology 48(4)
• Egg cases, ~16 eggs each, hiding near pipes and drains: University of Florida IFAS, EENY141 American Cockroach
• Squeezing through 1/10-inch cracks and surviving 900x their body weight: Jayaram and Full, PNAS 2016 (UC Berkeley)
• Running 1.5 m/s, about 50 body lengths per second: Full and Tu 1991, Journal of Experimental Biology
• Wind-sensing cerci and escaping toads: Camhi, Tom and Volman 1978, Journal of Comparative Physiology
• Living without a head: Scientific American, with entomologist Joseph Kunkel (UMass Amherst)
• "Come here" scents made by gut bacteria: Wada-Katsumata, Schal et al., PNAS 2015 (NC State)
